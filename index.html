RESPOND Self-Assessment

A reflective audit for any setting to review its own safeguarding practice against the RESPOND framework, from the first moments of recognition through to the systems that hold a response together.

This is a tool for honest reflection and shared conversation, not an inspection instrument. It is designed to complement statutory guidance and your existing policies, helping a setting see where practice is strong, where it is developing, and where to focus next. Rate each statement as it is now, not as you would like it to be. The gaps are the most useful part.

The maturity scale

EmergingRecognised in principle but not yet consistent in practice.
DevelopingIn place in parts of the setting or with some people, but uneven.
EstablishedConsistent and understood across the setting and reliably done.
EmbeddedLived and felt, sustained under pressure, and routinely reviewed.

The Escalation of Unseen Harm

The RESPOND model that describes how a concern intensifies through six predictable stages when early, subtle indicators are not acted upon. It is the reason the relational steps matter so much.

Risk does not stay still when it goes unrecognised, it accumulates. When the helix fragments, when Recognise does not lead to Engage, when Pause is skipped, or when Notify never happens, a student moves down these stages. Each one maps to the RESPOND step whose breakdown allows the harm to deepen. Intervening early, at stages one or two, is what prevents a stage six breakdown. Use your ratings above to ask honestly: at which stage would our practice currently catch this student?

The shift this asks for: from reacting to crisis, to noticing the quiet, cumulative signs early, the work of Recognise, Engage and Support, so that fewer students ever reach the later stages unseen.

Action plan

Choose which areas to turn into actions. Every statement you rated Emerging or Developing appears below. Tick the ones to include, add the detail, then export a branded Word document for your programme plan or governor report.

A recommended action is suggested for each, drawn from the RESPOND toolkit. Edit it to fit your setting.

RESPOND is a seven-step duty of care workflow, Recognise, Engage, Support, Pause, Offer, Notify, Document, powered by the ACT mindset (Active Intervention, Contextual Safeguarding, Trauma-Informed Practice). Pause is the bridge between the relational steps and the systems steps. The steps are a toolkit to move between, not a fixed checklist.
