RESPOND Self-Assessment

A reflective audit for any setting to review its own safeguarding practice against the RESPOND framework, from the first moments of recognition through to the systems that hold a response together.

This is a tool for honest reflection and shared conversation, not a compliance test. It is designed to complement statutory guidance and your existing policies, helping a setting see where practice is strong, where it is developing, and where to focus next. Rate each statement as it is now, not as you would like it to be. The gaps are the most useful part.

The maturity scale

The Escalation of Unseen Harm

The RESPOND model that describes how a concern intensifies through six predictable stages when early, subtle indicators are not acted upon. It is the reason the relational steps matter so much.

Risk does not stay still when it goes unrecognised, it accumulates. Each stage below maps to the RESPOND step whose strength determines whether a student is caught there. Strong practice early, at Recognise, Engage and Support, catches a student before harm deepens. Where early practice is weaker, a student is more likely to pass unseen until a later stage. The regulatory and evidence base

RESPOND is driven by regulation, research and evidence. This section does not ask whether you comply, it shows how the practice you have just assessed demonstrates the duties in the statutory framework. Compliance follows the work, it is the consequence of doing the right thing well.

Each RESPOND area below expands to show the statutory duties and guidance that the practice maps onto, with a prompt to help you locate the evidence you would want to show during an audit. Independent schools must meet the Independent School Standards and have regard to the named statutory guidance. "Have regard to" means a school can demonstrate it either follows the guidance or has good reason to depart from it.

Action plan

Choose which areas to turn into actions. Every statement you rated Emerging or Developing appears below. Tick the ones to include, add the detail, then export a branded Word document for your programme plan or governor report.

A recommended action is suggested for each, drawn from the RESPOND toolkit. Edit it to fit your setting.

RESPOND is a seven-step duty of care workflow, Recognise, Engage, Support, Pause, Offer, Notify, Document, powered by the ACT mindset (Active Intervention, Contextual Safeguarding, Trauma-Informed Practice). Pause is the bridge between the relational steps and the systems steps. The steps are a toolkit to move between, not a fixed checklist.
