RESPOND Self-Assessment

A reflective audit for any setting to review its own safeguarding practice against the RESPOND framework, from the first moments of recognition through to the systems that hold a response together.

How to use this tool

This is a tool for honest reflection and shared conversation, not a compliance test. It is designed to complement statutory guidance and your existing policies, helping a setting see where practice is strong, where it is developing, and where to focus next. Rate each statement as it is now, not as you would like it to be. The gaps are the most useful part.

Statutory floor  What the floor marker means

Some statements are marked as a statutory floor. These reflect duties that should be met now, not developmental aims. Maturity is the journey, but it sits above a floor that must always be met.

A statutory-floor item rated Emerging or Developing is treated as a priority in your results and plan. This tool demonstrates how your practice meets these duties and shows where attention is needed; it does not certify compliance, which remains a matter for your own judgement.

The Escalation of Unseen Harm

How a concern intensifies through six stages when early signs are missed, and where your current practice would tend to catch a student.

How this works

The RESPOND model describes how a concern intensifies through six predictable stages when early, subtle indicators are not acted upon. It is the reason the relational steps matter so much.

Risk does not stay still when it goes unrecognised, it accumulates. Each stage below maps to the RESPOND step whose strength determines whether a student is caught there. Strong practice early, at Recognise, Engage and Support, catches a student before harm deepens. Where early practice is weaker, a student is more likely to pass unseen until a later stage. How to read this section

RESPOND is driven by regulation, research and evidence. This section does not ask whether you comply, it shows how the practice you have just assessed demonstrates the duties in the statutory framework. Compliance follows the work, it is the consequence of doing the right thing well.

Each RESPOND area below expands to show the statutory duties and guidance that the practice maps onto, with a prompt to help you locate the evidence you would want to show during an audit. Independent schools must meet the Independent School Standards and have regard to the named statutory guidance. "Have regard to" means a school can demonstrate it either follows the guidance or has good reason to depart from it.

AI guidance and data protection

This reflection is generated by AI to support professional thinking. It is guidance only, it can be imperfect, and it does not replace statutory guidance, your safeguarding policy, or the judgement of your designated safeguarding lead. Always check it against your own professional knowledge.

When you generate a reflection, your ratings, notes and any draft actions are sent securely to the model to produce the summary, then returned. Nothing is stored, logged or retained on the server, and nothing is used to train any model. No personal data about students or staff is required by this tool, so please keep notes free of names or identifying details.

Everything else you enter stays in this browser on your own device and is never transmitted. See the foot of the page for more on how your progress is saved.

How the action plan works

Every statement you rated Emerging or Developing appears below. Tick the ones to include and add the detail you want to record.

A recommended action is suggested for each, drawn from the RESPOND toolkit. Edit it to fit your setting.

RESPOND is a seven-step duty of care workflow, Recognise, Engage, Support, Pause, Offer, Notify, Document, powered by the ACT mindset (Active Intervention, Contextual Safeguarding, Trauma-Informed Practice). Pause is the bridge between the relational steps and the systems steps. The steps are a toolkit to move between, not a fixed checklist.
